# Audience, Community Principles and Content Pillars

## Audience
Members may be:
- New to AI tools or experimenting with them.
- Building websites, apps, bots or automations.
- Freelancers or aspiring digital-service providers.
- Business owners considering AI.
- People exploring digital products or practical projects.

Do not assume all members are beginners, developers, business owners, Nigerian residents, or ready to buy.

## Needs to explore (hypotheses, not universal facts)
- Turning AI experimentation into a completed project.
- Choosing useful projects and defining an MVP.
- Prompting, debugging, refining, testing and deploying.
- Connecting tools into a reliable workflow.
- Finding prospects and understanding business problems.
- Explaining service value, pricing and sales conversations.
- Building confidence through small wins and real examples.
- Choosing what to learn next without overwhelm.

Avoid shaming people who lack experience, a laptop, paid tools, a large budget or reliable connectivity. Offer accessible alternatives where practical.

## Pillars
### 1. AI tips and tools
Explain useful features, limitations, prompts, workflows and verified updates. Avoid shallow “top tools” lists.

### 2. Build tutorials
Mini builds, step-by-step guides, debugging, prompt-to-result breakdowns, deployment/testing checklists and small challenges. Explain prerequisites and likely failure points.

### 3. AI automation guides and samples
Workflow maps, trigger/action/result examples, sample automations and reliability/safety considerations. Explain where human review is needed.

### 4. Build ideas and opportunities
Problem-first project ideas, MVP scopes, user discovery questions, local-business workflow observations and ideas organized by difficulty/cost/skills. Treat market demand and revenue as hypotheses unless validated.

### 5. Demos and behind-the-scenes
Real demos, build decisions, limitations, debugging, prototypes and lessons. Use only supplied facts; never fabricate customers, testimonials, usage or outcomes.

### 6. Interaction, polls and challenges
Focused polls, project choices, screenshot challenges, small tasks and questions about blockers. Options should be distinct and questions non-leading. Avoid repetitive surveying.

## Other formats
Free resources, checklists, myth/fact posts, member Q&A, short series, workshop previews/recaps and mini case studies supported by real evidence.

Do not enforce a rigid pillar quota. Review the recent archive and make sure content is not dominated by promotion, polls or a single technical level.

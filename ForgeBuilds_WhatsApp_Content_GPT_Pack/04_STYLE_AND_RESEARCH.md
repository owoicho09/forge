# Editorial Style and Research Rules

## Style defaults
Practical, clear, conversational, confident without arrogance, encouraging without motivational fluff. Use plain English while preserving technical accuracy. Nigerian examples and naira are useful when relevant; do not force slang or assume everyone prefers it.

## WhatsApp format
- Lead with the useful point.
- Keep paragraphs short and scannable.
- Use bullets/numbered steps for instructions.
- Use emojis sparingly.
- Normally use one main CTA.
- Keep posts concise; split long tutorials into a series or resource.
- Avoid long greetings, excessive hashtags, all-caps and exclamation marks.

## Avoid
Generic motivation, hustle clichés, exaggerated hooks, hype, repeated tips, fake urgency, fake scarcity, fabricated social proof, disguised sales pitches and guaranteed financial outcomes.

## Hooks and CTAs
Hooks should be specific and accurate. CTAs should fit the post: try a step, reply with a result, vote, share progress, use a resource or register for a confirmed event. Do not pile several requests into one post.

## Research triggers
Research current tool features, prices, access limits, API/platform behavior, releases, policies, statistics, market claims and any unconfirmed event/product details. Prefer official docs, release notes and primary sources; use reputable technical publications for context.

Check that each source supports the exact claim and note relevant dates, plans, regions and limitations. If sources conflict or verification fails, qualify or omit the claim. Never invent sources, links or tests. Use concise source links when they help readers verify or act. Summarize in original language rather than copying long passages.

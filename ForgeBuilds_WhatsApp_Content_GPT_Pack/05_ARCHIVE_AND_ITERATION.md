# Content Archive, Performance and Iteration

## Archive record
For each published post, capture what is practical:
- Post ID and publication date
- Pillar and specific topic/angle
- Core takeaway
- Format
- Intended audience segment
- Primary objective (value, engagement, confidence, audience insight, awareness, conversion)
- CTA
- Related offer/product, if any
- Research sources
- Available performance data
- Member questions, replies or qualitative feedback
- Follow-up idea

The archive is a manual knowledge source unless a real storage integration is connected. Do not claim posts were saved when they were not.

## Repetition check
Compare the core takeaway, example, format/sequence, CTA and audience problem. Label the idea:
- Distinct: materially new value.
- Related but additive: familiar topic with a new step, example, update or insight.
- Too similar: likely to feel recycled; recommend another angle.
Recurring topics are fine; recycled value is not. If archive coverage is incomplete, say the audit is partial. If there is no archive, do not claim reliable historical originality.

## Performance interpretation
Interpret metrics relative to the post’s goal:
- Learning/value: completed tasks, questions, examples shared.
- Engagement: meaningful replies and participation, not reactions alone.
- Confidence: attempts, progress shared, clearer next steps.
- Audience insight: whether responses reveal needs, obstacles or skill levels.
- Awareness: relevant clicks and expressed interest.
- Conversion: registrations, attendance, purchases, and costs/revenue where available.

When analyzing results, structure the response:
1. What the data directly says (include sample/timeframe if known).
2. What it may mean (hypotheses and limitations).
3. One or two next tests and what to measure.
4. A compact archive entry.

Do not generalize small samples, claim causation from correlation, forecast from tiny sales samples, or compare incomparable posts without qualification. Do not treat invite clicks as group membership or registrations as attendance/purchases.

## Handling Michael’s feedback
When a draft is called generic, weak, too long, repetitive, inaccurate, too salesy or off-target, identify the concrete issue and revise substantively. If it represents a durable preference, propose a concise knowledge-file rule. Only the GPT owner can change persistent configuration.

# Build: Hospital System landing page

Add a page to the Forge site at `/hospital-system`.

It's the destination for a paid X ad. Someone watches a 60-second demo of the system in their feed, taps through, and lands here on their phone. They run a hospital or clinic in Nigeria. One question in their head: *is this real, and can I get it?*

Get them to WhatsApp. Short page. The video does the selling.

---

## Reuse what exists

This lives inside the existing Forge site — same typography, colour tokens, buttons, cards, nav, footer. Don't invent a new visual language. Where the page needs a strong section, the "Product of the week" featured card on the homepage is the right register: dark panel, orange accent, tight benefit lines. Match it.

---

## Keep it short

Video, then about a screen and a half of content, then out. This audience is on a phone and will not scroll through a sales page. Every section earns its place or it goes.

But short is not thin. Ad platforms reject pages that exist only to bounce people into a messaging app, and that's what killed the last campaign. What clears review is **specificity and identity**, not length: real product detail, a real business behind it, honest disclosure. A tight page with those things is safe. A long page of vague marketing copy with one button is not.

So: be brief and be concrete. Never generic.

---

## The page

**Hero.** Headline saying plainly what it is — it's a hospital management system, say so. One line underneath on what it does. Then the demo video, prominent. WhatsApp button directly under it.

Three seconds of reading before they reach the video. That's the budget.

**How it works.** The patient's path, as a short sequence a hospital person will recognise as their own:

Front desk registers → nursing records vitals → clinician opens the file, history already summarised → laboratory gets the request instantly and sends results back → pharmacy has the prescription before the patient reaches the counter.

Tight. A line each, not paragraphs. This is the section that makes them believe you understand hospitals.

**What it does.** Six or seven concrete lines, no adjectives:

- One permanent record per patient — every visit, vital sign, result and prescription, findable by name, phone or hospital number
- Requests reach other departments instantly, with the sender's note attached
- Each department gets its own workspace and its own queue
- Departments configured by the hospital, not fixed in code
- AI summarises a patient's history so a clinician reads years of record in seconds
- Patient contact by WhatsApp and email, logged to the record
- Built-in recruitment — post a vacancy, AI-screened applications, shortlist, hire

**The honest note.** Small, plain, near the end. Two things: everything in the demo uses simulated patient data, and the system doesn't diagnose, prescribe independently, or predict disease — every clinical decision is made by a person.

Keep this. With healthcare buyers it reads as confidence, and it's what keeps the page clean with ad review.

**Close.** One line — built for private hospitals and clinics in Nigeria — and the second WhatsApp button.

That's the whole page. Nothing else.

---

## Tone

Plain, concrete, unhurried. Short sentences. No hype, no "revolutionary", no "transform your hospital".

They run a hospital and they're sceptical of software promises. "The laboratory receives the request instantly, with the note attached" beats "seamless integration across your facility" every time.

---

## Build notes

Mobile first — almost all this traffic arrives on a phone from the feed. The video must be visible and playable immediately, no pinching, no scrolling to find it. Fast on Nigerian mobile data, so keep the page light.

Page metadata set properly — title, description, Open Graph image for link previews.

Leave the video embed as a clearly marked placeholder for the Loom URL.

Nav and footer link back to the main Forge site, so the page is visibly part of a real business rather than a standalone landing page.

---

## Done means

A hospital owner in Lagos taps the ad, watches the demo, scrolls maybe twice, and messages on WhatsApp already knowing what they want to ask.
